## Scope

Grades the visual design of a user interface that an AI tool generated from a text prompt, as seen in two full-page screenshots taken after the page loads: one at 1280 px wide and one at 375 px wide. Tall pages are cut at 3200 px.

### Not graded

- Interaction and task completion. FlowEval (arXiv 2605.04165) covers interaction flows.
- Whether the tool's own stated design reasoning matches what it built. Design Theater (arXiv 2607.22928) covers that.
- Accessibility in lived use, performance, code quality and cultural fit.
- Anything that needs a click, a hover or a scroll beyond the two screenshots.

## Rules for the grader

1. Grade what is in the two screenshots. Do not open the page.
2. Give the holistic grade first, before any dimension. Do not change it afterwards.
3. Use whole numbers from 1 to 5. A 2 or a 4 means between the two anchors beside it. It never means almost.
4. Grade each dimension on its own. A weak hierarchy does not lower the typography grade.
5. Can't tell is allowed. It is recorded as missing, never as a 3.
6. Mark unsure when you could argue the grade a point either way. The share of unsure grades is published.
7. Measured facts are shown beside the screenshots. They inform the grade. They do not set it.
8. Do not try to guess which tool made the interface. The order is shuffled and the tool is hidden.
9. A page with no deliberate design choices, such as default browser styles, grades 1 on D1 (no hierarchy was chosen), D2 (no layout was chosen) and D3 (default type). D4 grades 2 if text passes contrast and nothing clashes, because having no palette is not the same as a clash. D5 and D6 are graded on what is there.
10. Stand-in imagery: flat colour shapes, gradient blocks or grey boxes standing where the brief calls for photographs or illustrations count as unfinished imagery. D6 is at most 3. They do not lower H, which assumes real images are dropped in. Abstract shapes that are plainly the design, not a placeholder for a picture, are not stand-ins.
11. If the 375 px screenshot shows content running off the side of the screen, or text that cannot be read, H is at most 3, however good the 1280 px view is.
12. Layout faults at either width are graded in D2 only. D6 does not penalise them again.
13. H is about the design. Assume real images and copy are dropped in. Missing assets lower D6 only.

## Scale

Whole numbers 1 to 5. Anchors are written for 1, 3 and 5. A 2 or a 4 means between the neighbouring anchors.

## H. Client-ready (graded first)

**Would you deliver this to a client as it stands?**

- **1.** No. I would start again.
- **3.** Not yet. A day of design fixes to spacing, type, colour or hierarchy and I would. Assume real images and copy are dropped in.
- **5.** Yes. I would send it as it stands.

*UI-Bench asks a pairwise version of this: 'Which project would you be more likely to deliver to a client?' This is the absolute version. The numbers are not directly comparable.*

## D1. Hierarchy

**Where does the eye go first, second and third, and does that order match what the page is for?**

- **1.** No clear first read. Everything has similar weight, or the loudest thing is the wrong thing.
- **3.** A clear first read, but the second and third compete, or the order is partly wrong for the purpose.
- **5.** One clear first read, then a deliberate path. Emphasis is spent in one or two places and the rest stays quiet.

Look for: size and weight contrast; what the call to action competes with; whether the headline or an image wins.

## D2. Layout and spacing

**Are alignment, grid, spacing and density consistent, at both widths?**

- **1.** Elements misalign or collide, gaps are inconsistent, or the mobile view is the desktop squeezed until it breaks.
- **3.** Mostly aligned on a recognisable grid. Spacing is consistent in places. Generic stacking. Mobile works but is cramped or loose.
- **5.** A consistent spacing scale and strong alignment. Density suits the content. The mobile view is a considered layout, not a squeezed desktop.

Look for: left edges that line up; repeated gaps; what the 375 px view does with columns.

## D3. Typography

**Do scale, weight, pairing, line length and line spacing make the text easy and pleasant to read?**

- **1.** Default or clashing type, no visible scale, lines far too long or short, text hard to read.
- **3.** Readable and coherent. A scale exists but is timid or uneven.
- **5.** A clear scale with purposeful contrast. Pairing, or a single family, is used with intent. Comfortable line length and spacing at both widths.

Look for: number of sizes in use; heading against body contrast; line length of body text.

## D4. Colour and contrast

**Is the palette cohesive and restrained, does colour have a job, and is text legible against its background?**

- **1.** The palette clashes or is a stock default gradient. Body text fails contrast.
- **3.** Cohesive but safe. Contrast passes. The accent has no clear role.
- **5.** A restrained palette where accent and neutral each have a role. Contrast passes wherever it matters. Colour supports the hierarchy.

Look for: the measured contrast ratio beside the screenshot; how many hues compete; whether the accent marks the action.

## D5. Brief fit and distinctiveness

**Does it look made for this brief, or for any brief?**

- **1.** Could be any site. It ignores the brief's mood, audience or content.
- **3.** Fits the brief's category but is interchangeable with many others of its kind.
- **5.** Specific to the brief. Something in it would not appear on the output for a neighbouring brief.

Look for: the mood the brief asked for; choices that only make sense for this subject; template smell.

## D6. Finish

**How complete and consistent is it in the small things: content, imagery, icons, components, edges?**

- **1.** Broken images, placeholder text, overflow, or visibly unfinished sections.
- **3.** Complete, with visible rough edges: generic icons, repeated filler, inconsistent components.
- **5.** No visible rough edges. Components are consistent. The content reads as real.

Look for: the measured defect flags beside the screenshot; stand-in imagery (rule 10); repeated filler; mismatched buttons or cards.

## Crosswalk

Overlap with TASTE (arXiv 2605.20731) is deliberate, so results can be compared. TASTE grades AI-generated graphic design (logos, posters, flyers), not coded interfaces.

- **H Client-ready:** TASTE 'UI+Ad Preference (holistic)' and 'Preference (holistic)'. UI-Bench's client-delivery question.
- **D1 Hierarchy:** TASTE 'Visual Hierarchy'.
- **D2 Layout and spacing:** No direct TASTE criterion. TASTE 'Spatial Accuracy' checks the layout against the prompt's description, which is a different question.
- **D3 Typography:** TASTE 'Typography' (two cohorts: type craft, and whether the demanded text renders correctly).
- **D4 Colour and contrast:** TASTE 'Color Harmony'. TASTE 'Color Accuracy' checks requested colours appear, which is a different question.
- **D5 Brief fit and distinctiveness:** TASTE 'Mood and Tone Match'.
- **D6 Finish:** No TASTE criterion. TASTE records hallucination flags as No, Minor or Major.
